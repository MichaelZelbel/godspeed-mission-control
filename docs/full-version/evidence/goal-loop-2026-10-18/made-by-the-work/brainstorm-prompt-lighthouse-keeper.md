# Brainstorm Prompts: The Lighthouse Keeper

## Places That Feel Significant (5 prompts)

1. What is the most isolated place you have ever visited or lived? Describe three sensory details about being there alone — what did you hear when nothing else was moving, what did the air smell like, what surface did your hands rest on?

2. Imagine standing on a rocky coast at 3 AM. Not the postcard version — the real version. Salt wind stinging your face, water crashing below, no streetlights for miles. What goes through your mind when darkness feels absolute and you're the only person awake within twenty miles?

3. Think of a room where you felt safe but trapped. Could you leave physically but not emotionally? How does that contradiction feel in your body? (This becomes the lighthouse keeper's emotional geography.)

4. You're watching someone tend something that others rely on but rarely notice — a gardener, a night-shift worker, a nurse. They don't look heroic. What mundane tasks keep repeating that eventually matter more than anything dramatic they might do?

5. There's a place you've been told belongs to someone else — a family home inherited, a workplace that feels owned by rules not people. What's the quietest rebellion you performed there just to prove you had agency?

## People Whose Contradictions Fascinate You (5 prompts)

1. Take someone real whose job requires constant attention to detail while everyone around them rushes past without noticing. Now give them a hobby that involves complete abandonment of control — pottery throwing, improvisational music, rock climbing. How do these two modes coexist in one person? What tensions arise?

2. Someone you know speaks differently with different people — softer with elders, sharper with authority figures, playful with children. When they speak with no one they know, what voice emerges? Is it louder or quieter than all the others? What would a stranger think hearing it unfiltered?

3. A person who appears calm under pressure but secretly loses sleep over small things — a misplaced object, an unanswered email, a slight tone in conversation. They carry a private alarm clock in their chest. What fills those nighttime hours?

4. Someone whose competence in one area makes them insecure in another — a brilliant professional who cannot cook, a fearless traveler who panics in crowds, a generous giver who hoards time. Which skill does their insecurity cost them? What would happen if that skill finally caught up?

5. Pick someone older whom you observe from outside. They do one thing every day that seems pointless to strangers — arranging stones, collecting shells, writing the same paragraph over and over. What if that "pointless" ritual is actually the anchor holding everything else together? What happens to them if they miss a single day?
