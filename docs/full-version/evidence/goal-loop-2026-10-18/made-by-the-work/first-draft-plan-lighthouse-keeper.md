# First Draft Plan: The Lighthouse Keeper
## A structured path from zero to completed first draft

Based on Seth Fried's Skillshare sequence, verified against five documented cases. Target: 5,000 words across 6 sessions over 3-5 days.

---

## Section 1: Brainstorm Lists (~300 words)
**Time estimate:** 30 minutes
**From Seth Fried's Step 1 + Robert Mangeat's material-gathering method**

### Places That Feel Significant
(Using prepared prompts at drafts/brainstorm-prompt-lighthouse-keeper.md)
- [ ] Most isolated place you've ever visited: sensory details of being alone there
- [ ] Rocky coast at 3 AM: absolute darkness, only sounds of water
- [ ] Room where you felt safe but trapped: physical vs emotional geography
- [ ] Someone tending something others rely on but rarely notice: mundane tasks that matter
- [ ] A place told to belong to someone else: quietest rebellion performed

### People Whose Contradictions Fascinate You
(Fried hybrid character method + Bender interiority building)
- [ ] Constant attention to detail + complete abandonment of control hobby
- [ ] Speaks differently with different people: unfiltered voice
- [ ] Calm under pressure but loses sleep over small things: nighttime hours
- [ ] Competence in one area breeds insecurity in another: what does it cost?
- [ ] Older person doing "pointless" ritual: what happens if they miss a day?

**Target output:** Pick your top 3 places and 3 people. Write one paragraph per choice.

---

## Section 2: Freewrite Burst (~900 words)
**Time estimate:** 30 minutes continuous, no stopping
**From Seth Fried's Step 2 + Hemingway's "first draft is always expletive"**

Using your chosen brainstorm element as starting point:
1. Set timer for 30 minutes
2. Open to a blank page
3. Start writing — DO NOT stop, DELETE, BACKTRACK, or REVIEW
4. If stuck, skip ahead to a different scene or moment
5. When timer rings, STOP even mid-sentence

**Rules:** No editing. No reviewing. No correcting spelling. No looking back. If you catch yourself reading what you wrote, start the timer again.

---

## Section 3: Hybrid Character Sketch (~400 words)
**Time estimate:** 20 minutes
**From Seth Fried's Step 3 + Karen Bender's interiority building seminar**

Build the lighthouse keeper using Fried's hybrid method:
1. Take ONE real person whose contradictions fascinate you (from brainstorm section 2)
2. Give them their opposite traits (e.g., if they're outgoing → make them quietly intense; if organized → make them chaotic inside)
3. Place this hybrid in the lighthouse setting
4. Describe: name, appearance (derived from real person), key contradiction, motivation

**Add Bender's verb-driven prose (Claim 7):** Describe what the character DOES, not how they look.

---

## Section 4: Opening Section (~600 words)
**Time estimate:** 30 minutes
**From Karen Bender's two-question diagnostic (Claim 5)**

Write the opening of your story. Before drafting, identify:
- **Question A:** What question does my opening ask about the present?
- **Question B:** What question does my opening ask about what lies beneath?

These must be IN PRODUCTIVE FRICTION — answering one complicates the other.

Then write 600+ words of opening scene. Use:
- Specific verbs (Bender Claim 7)
- At least one Octopus Moment: a memory, premonition, or cultural reference that reaches beyond the immediate scene
- Escalation: each beat pushes further than the last (Saunders Claim 11)

---

## Section 5: Discovery-Written Narrative (~2,800 words)
**Time estimate:** 2-3 hours total, broken into multiple sessions
**From Robert Mangeot's discovery writing + Gaiman's pay-by-word discipline**

You don't know where the story ends yet. That's normal (Mangeot). Write forward, connecting beginning to end as you go.

Daily targets:
- Day 1 after opening: 800 words minimum
- Day 2: 800 words minimum  
- Day 3: 800+ words to reach target

Use the craft-reference card at templates/craft-reference-lighthouse-keeper.md whenever you're unsure what to do next. Reference it BEFORE pausing to think.

**Checkpoints per session:**
- [ ] Did I escalate? (refused to repeat beats)
- [ ] Did I use verbs more than adjectives?
- [ ] Did I add at least one Octopus Moment?
- [ ] Does the second draft version of this read as intentional?

## When Things Go Wrong (PLAYBOOK Where It Fails)

**Blank page paralysis:** Forgiving the first draft being messy (Patchett). Write badly. Everything is allowed.

**Editing during draft:** Every word costs money (Gaiman). If you catch yourself polishing a sentence, jump to the next one. Save edits for draft two.

**Getting sidetracked by voice:** Stay on the straight line (Mangeot). Voice gets polished later; first draft is just getting the story down.

**Not knowing the ending:** Discovery writing is validated practice. Let the story talk back to you (Saunders). Don't plan what comes next.
