## What they do

The levers in order of leverage for going from zero to first draft:

1. **Brainstorm lists**: Write about time/place/person without filtering. Seth Fried's sequence starts here. Evidence: his entire course structure, later published in The New Yorker, McSweeney's, NPR Short Cuts. This is where every case begins.

2. **Freewrite without editing**: Generate raw material — Fried does 900+ words, Mangeot did 1,200/day for four days. Hemingway: "The first draft is always expletive." Patchett: "forgive the first draft." Rule: complete draft before touching revision.

3. **Build hybrid characters**: Mix known people with imagined qualities (Fried's method). Characters come from reality + opposite traits. Not invented from scratch.

4. **Discovery writing**: Don't plan the ending. Manageot: "you don't know what it's about until it's done." Saunders: "the story is talking back to you." Outline-averse approach validated by practitioners.

5. **Openings with two questions**: Bender's diagnostic — every opening should establish two questions in productive friction. Practical tool that can be applied even before drafting.

6. **Octopus Moment**: Add a sentence reaching beyond the immediate scene — memory, premonition, or perception connecting present to larger world. Creates depth without new scenes.

7. **Escalation discipline**: Refuse to repeat beats (Saunders). Each new beat raises stakes. Verb-driven prose over adjectives (Bender).

## In what order

1. Brainstorm: create three lists — places that feel significant, people whose contradictions fascinate you, moments of tension you've witnessed
2. Pick one element from any list and freewrite for 30 minutes without stopping, without correcting, without looking back
3. Build one character by taking someone real and adding their opposite trait
4. Start writing the story from any point (not necessarily beginning); discover where it goes via discovery writing
5. When stuck, apply Bender's two-question diagnostic to any opening section
6. Insert Octopus Moments at natural pause points — memory, premonition, cultural reference
7. Keep escalating; refuse repetition; cut passive descriptions, prefer verbs
8. When you reach a sense of "end," stop. First draft complete. Then begin revision.

This maps Seth Fried's exact sequence but adds Bender's technique at the right insertion point.

## Where it fails

**The blank page**: Most common failure. Everyone stops at step 0. Prevention: forgive the first draft being messy (Patchett), use freewriting (no editing allowed in session 1).

**Perfectionism during drafting**: Trying to make every sentence beautiful before moving forward. Signs: reading paragraph twice, deleting half-written text, spending more than 10 min on single sentence. Fix: Gaiman's "pay by the word" mindset — treat every word as costing money, forces economy and forward motion.

**Getting sidetracked by voice**: Mangeot says writers get distracted trying to find a unique voice in early drafts instead of staying "on the straight line." Fix: second draft is where voice gets polished; first draft is just getting the story down.

**Not knowing where the ending goes**: Beginners try to plan everything upfront. Evidence: Mangeat wrote forward without knowing the end; Saunders confirms "story talks back." Fix: acceptance of discovery writing.

## Unknown

1. **How much brainstorming is enough?** Fried produces lists then freewrites. But how many lists trigger good freewriting? Is there a threshold (3 lists? 5?) or is it qualitative (when something feels interesting)? This would be settled by trying and measuring word output per brainstorm session.

2. **Does the Bender two-question technique work for all genres/openings?** Strong consensus among craft teachers but untested empirically. The claim could fail for stories that rely on atmosphere rather than question-driven openings.

3. **Optimal freewriting duration?** 30 minutes? 60? One continuous burst vs. multiple sessions? Evidence suggests longer = more output but diminishing returns after some point. Needs testing.
