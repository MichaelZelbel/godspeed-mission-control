# Subtitle Design

## Separate the jobs

The title earns attention, memory, recognition, or curiosity. The subtitle explains enough of the value to make the title commercially useful.

A subtitle can perform these jobs:

- Clarify the outcome
- Identify the audience
- Reveal the mechanism or thesis
- Remove an important objection
- Define the scope or category
- Add credible specificity
- Signal the book's format or practicality

A strong subtitle usually performs two or three jobs. Trying to perform all seven creates keyword soup.

## Match the subtitle to the title

- **Evocative or metaphorical title:** Make the subtitle concrete and category-clear.
- **Direct outcome title:** Use the subtitle to add mechanism, audience, or credibility.
- **Technical category title:** Use the subtitle to promise application and differentiation.
- **Contrarian title:** Use the subtitle to explain the new alternative rather than repeat the attack.
- **Named-framework title:** Explain what the framework does and for whom.

## Useful structures

Treat these as scaffolds, not final copy.

1. `How to [wanted result] without [feared cost]`
2. `A practical [guide/playbook/system] for [audience] to [result]`
3. `Build or use [mechanism] to [result]`
4. `[Verb], [verb], and [verb] [object or domain]`
5. `Why [common belief] fails and how [new approach] works`
6. `[Outcome] for [audience] through [mechanism]`
7. `Turn [existing asset] into [valuable result]`
8. `Design, govern, and deploy [thing] across [scope]`

## Subtitle discipline

- Start with roughly 8 to 18 words as a useful discipline, not a law.
- Use plain language and concrete nouns.
- Do not repeat the main title's key claim unless repetition is necessary for meaning.
- Do not stack empty adjectives such as revolutionary, ultimate, proven, comprehensive, or game-changing.
- Do not stuff search keywords into unnatural grammar.
- Avoid two subtitles joined together by several punctuation marks.
- Make sure the subtitle accurately describes the manuscript the reader will receive.
- Read the complete title and subtitle aloud as one unit.

## Diagnostic examples

### Repetition

Weak:

> `Own Your AI: How to Own Your AI and Make It Yours`

Better:

> `Own Your AI: Build a Portable Context System That Knows You and Works Your Way`

### Metaphor without clarity

Weak:

> `The Portable Mind: A Journey into Tomorrow`

Better:

> `The Portable Mind: Turn Your Prompts, Preferences, and Knowledge into a Personal AI You Control`

### Technical title without benefit

Weak:

> `AI Agents for SAP: Concepts and Considerations`

Better:

> `AI Agents for SAP: Design, Govern, and Deploy Reliable Agents Across SAP Processes`

## Marketplace length checks

Before publication, verify the current platform rules. Amazon KDP currently requires the combined title and subtitle to remain within 200 characters and warns that readers may skim past long titles over roughly 60 characters. Treat the 60-character point as a browsing heuristic, not a universal law.
