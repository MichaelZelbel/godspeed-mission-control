# Output Templates

Use the shortest template that still supports the user's decision.

## Full creation mode

```markdown
# Book-title strategy

## Strategic brief
- Reader:
- Present situation:
- Wanted result:
- Distinctive mechanism or thesis:
- Proof and claim limit:
- Voice:
- Market language to avoid:

## Candidate directions
| # | Title | Subtitle | Architecture | Core appeal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ... | ... | ... | ... |

## Scored finalists
### 1. [Title]
*[Subtitle]*

- Score: [x]/100
- Cold-reader interpretation:
- Why it works:
- Main risk:
- Best use or audience:

[Repeat for 3 to 5 finalists]

## Leading recommendation
**[Title]**  
*[Subtitle]*

Explain why this pair is the strongest strategic hypothesis. Name one condition that could change the recommendation.

## Validation before locking it
Give the smallest useful market, collision, and reader test.
```

Present roughly 8 to 12 distinct candidate directions and 3 to 5 finalists unless the user asks for another amount. Do not reveal the private rough pool.

```markdown

|---|---:|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

*[Subtitle]*

[Reasoning and unresolved risk]
```

## Refinement mode

```markdown
# Refined directions around "[chosen title]"

| # | Title | Subtitle | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ... | ... | ... |

## Best refinement
**[Title]**  
*[Subtitle]*

[Why it improves the original without losing its strategic core]
```

## Quick-ideas mode

```markdown
| # | Title | Subtitle | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ... | ... | ... |

**Strongest three:** [1], [2], and [3].
```

Even in quick mode, ensure the options represent different strategic architectures. Do not pad the answer with weak variations.

```markdown
|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

## Reader-test shortlist
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...

## Test questions and decision rule
[Concise test plan]
```

## Style rules

- Write plainly and decisively.
- Explain the strategic reason, not a vague claim that a title is "catchy."
- Distinguish evidence from inference.
- Do not call any title a guaranteed bestseller.
- Do not force a single winner when two candidates serve different commercial jobs.
