# Worked Examples

The examples are illustrative. Availability and rights have not been checked.

## Example 1: Personal AI context system

### Brief

- Reader: Knowledge workers and AI power users tired of re-explaining themselves to every model
- Result: A personal AI that knows their preferences, projects, and methods
- Mechanism: User-owned Markdown context containing prompts, skills, knowledge, and decisions
- Emotional payoff: Control, continuity, and independence
- Tone: Intelligent, calm, practical

### Generic output to reject

> `The Ultimate AI Productivity Blueprint`

Why it fails: It is interchangeable, uses stock AI-marketing language, does not express ownership or continuity, and says nothing distinctive about context.

### Stronger strategic directions

1. **Own Your AI**  
   *Build a Portable Context System That Knows You, Works Your Way, and Moves With You*

2. **The Portable Mind**  
   *Turn Your Prompts, Preferences, and Knowledge into a Personal AI You Control*

3. **Context Is the Advantage**  
   *How to Build a Personal AI That Improves With Every Project*

4. **Stop Starting Over With AI**  
   *Create a Reusable Markdown Brain for Your Prompts, Skills, Knowledge, and Decisions*

The four options make different strategic bets: ownership, metaphor, thesis, and problem escape.

## Example 2: Technical enterprise book

### Brief

- Reader: SAP architects, consultants, and enterprise AI leaders
- Result: Build useful, reliable agents in SAP environments
- Mechanism: Architecture, integration, governance, and practical patterns
- Tone: Credible and technical, not futuristic hype

### Strong category-clear pair

> **AI Agents for SAP**  
> *Design, Govern, and Deploy Reliable Agents Across SAP Processes*

Why it works: The main title maximizes category and search clarity. The subtitle converts a broad topic into three practical jobs and signals enterprise reliability.

### More visionary alternative

> **The Agentic SAP Enterprise**  
> *A Practical Architecture for AI Agents, Process Automation, and Human Control*

Main risk: "Agentic" may be familiar to specialists but less clear to some business readers. The subtitle must carry the practical meaning.

### Candidate to reject

> `SAP Reimagined: Unlocking the Future of Intelligent Transformation`

Why it fails: It is crowded with generic technology language, promises no concrete result, and could describe almost any SAP consulting presentation.

## Example 3: Consultant lead-generation book

### Brief

- Reader: Independent consultants with good expertise but inconsistent demand
- Result: More qualified conversations without constant cold outreach
- Mechanism: A referral and authority system
- Tone: Direct, useful, non-guru

### Distinct directions

1. **More Clients, Less Chasing**  
   *Build a Referral System That Starts Qualified Conversations Before You Reach Out*

2. **The Trusted Expert Loop**  
   *Turn Useful Ideas, Referrals, and Follow-Up into Consistent Consulting Demand*

3. **Stop Hunting for Clients**  
   *A Practical Authority System for Independent Consultants*

The first is benefit-led, the second mechanism-led, and the third pain-escape-led. Testing should determine which language most strongly matches the audience's self-image.

## Example 4: Audit of a promising title

Candidate:

> **An AI of Your Own**  
> *Build a personal AI that knows you, works your way, and runs without you*

### Strengths

- Ownership is emotionally resonant and differentiated from rented, generic AI experiences.
- The title is human, memorable, and not overloaded with technical jargon.
- The subtitle explains the practical promise and creates a satisfying three-part rhythm.

### Risks to test

- "Runs without you" can suggest autonomous operation, which may be broader than a book about context ownership.
- "An AI" may sound slightly abstract unless the subtitle quickly establishes a system the reader can build.
- Search and collision checks remain necessary.

### Possible calibrated refinement

> **An AI of Your Own**  
> *Build a Personal AI That Knows You, Works Your Way, and Keeps Getting Better*

This version trades autonomy for continuity and learning. Whether that is an improvement depends on the actual manuscript promise.
