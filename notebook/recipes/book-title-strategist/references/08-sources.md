## Direct-response book-title strategy

- Dean Jackson, *5 Book Title Formulas*: https://90mb-pub-lib.s3.amazonaws.com/5%20Book%20Title%20Formulas.pdf

Key use: Generate across materially different title categories, especially desired-state, outcome declaration, how-to benefit, information resource, and question-led titles. The source also discusses `Double Your Dating` as an outcome declaration.

- Unofficial third-party notes from Eben Pagan's *Altitude* program: https://www.mynotetakingnerd.com/Nerd-Reports/Get%20Altitude%20Super%20Report.pdf

Key use: Reader language, desired outcomes, narrow audience recognition, rhythm, repeated sounds, and memorability. Treat this as an unofficial summary rather than an authoritative transcript.

## Copywriting and positioning

- Corey Haines, Copywriting Agent Skill: https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/copywriting/SKILL.md

Key use: Clarity over cleverness, benefits over features, specificity over vagueness, customer language over company language, and honest claims.

- Built&Written, "AI Book Title Generator: Stand Out, Not Blend In": https://www.builtwritten.com/blog/ai-book-title-generator-stand-out-not-blend-in

Key use: Promise, person, proof, and landscape as inputs; differentiated generation rounds; title-subtitle separation; and validation with real readers.

## Marketplace rules

- Amazon KDP, "Books Titles & Editions": https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/GW7J4WEKBVU25YEC
- Amazon KDP, "Metadata Guidelines for Books": https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201097560
- Amazon KDP, "Make Your Book More Discoverable with Keywords": https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G201298500

Key use: Metadata accuracy, title-cover consistency, prohibited promotional material, combined title and subtitle length, and the browsing cost of excessively long titles. Recheck current rules before publication because platform policies can change.

## Why external testing matters

- Zikun Ye, Hema Yoganarasimhan, and Yufeng Zheng, "LOLA: LLM-Assisted Online Learning Algorithm for Content Experiments," *Marketing Science*: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2024.0990

Key use: Pure LLM approaches performed poorly at selecting the winning headline across a large archive of headline experiments. Headline clicks are not identical to book-title sales, but the evidence supports using LLM judgment to generate and narrow candidates rather than treating self-scoring as market proof.
