# About-You Template (Chapters 1 and 4)

In Chapter 1 the AI you already use writes a short briefing about you under five headings,
and Chapter 4 files it into `profile/about-me.md`. This template is the **fallback**: use it
if you are new to AI tools or the briefing came back thin or empty.

It uses the same five headings. Write it straight into `profile/about-me.md` in your
mission control. A few true lines under each heading are plenty.

```
# About me

## Who I am and what I do
[Your work and situation. One line on how your days usually go, and
anything that shapes your time.]

## The people who matter
[The handful your assistant should know, by role: team, clients, family.
Note who to loop in on what. Chapter 6 gives them a file of their own.]

## What I am focused on now
[Live priorities and the projects actually on your plate this month, with
deadlines. Chapter 7 gives these a file of their own.]

## How I like to be helped
[Answer first then why? Short bullets or full prose? Blunt or gentle? Ask
before assuming things?]

## My hard limits
[The things it should never do. Tone you hate, things it should never
suggest, words it should never use.]
```

## Filled example (a made-up freelance illustrator)

```
# About me

## Who I am and what I do
I'm Sam, a freelance illustrator working from home. I juggle 3 regular
clients plus one-off jobs. Mornings until 1pm are my drawing hours and I
protect them hard. Calls and admin go in the afternoon.

## The people who matter
My agent handles contracts. Three regular clients: a board-game studio, a
children's book publisher, and a coffee brand. My partner shares my
calendar.

## What I am focused on now
Finishing a 20-card illustration set, due end of the month. Raising my
rates for new clients. Getting a portfolio site live.

## How I like to be helped
Answer first, then the short why. Tight bullet lists. Ask before assuming
a deadline. If I'm overthinking, tell me.

## My hard limits
Never write in stiff corporate language. Never suggest I work through the
night. No exclamation marks in my client emails.
```

## Two rules from Chapter 1

**Keep credentials out.** Passwords, access keys, payment details and other
credentials never go in this file. Keep them in a password manager.

**A few accurate lines beat a complete page.** Mark anything you are unsure of
with (?), and leave a gap visible rather than filling it with a guess.
