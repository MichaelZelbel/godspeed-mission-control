# Capture Checklist (Chapter 10)

Pocket card. Tell your mission control once, during the work you are doing, and it saves
the fact for next time.

## The one rule: say it while you work

Mention what happened in the conversation where it matters, with who and what. Your mission
control saves the new fact with its source and date, then briefly confirms what it kept.
When a fact changes, it keeps the earlier version as history.

## What is worth saying

1. **A new agreement**
2. **A decision** (yours, or one made around you)
3. **Something you learned from a job**
4. **An instruction for how to work with you.** "Ask before sending anything in my name" is
   a rule to follow; "I liked that short answer" is feedback on one answer.

Not every sentence deserves a permanent record.

## Three quality rules

- Say **who and what**, not just what. "Budget doubled" will confuse you both in a month.
- **A hint stays a hint.** A possibility is saved as a possibility, never as an agreed job.
- **Private line:** keep passwords and access keys out of these conversations. A note about
  another person needs only the detail the work requires (Chapter 22).

## Where unclear notes wait

Unclear notes wait in `inbox/`, for example a deadline that could belong to two projects.
Your mission control finishes filing one when the answer becomes clear, and the weekly review
(Chapter 27) files what is clear and asks about the rest. Writing samples and other material
you want kept untouched can stay in `inbox/` too.

## Check that the note survives

Do this once, when you start:

- [ ] Tell it one harmless fact.
- [ ] In a fresh session, ask about that fact and have the assistant show the saved source.
- [ ] If it cannot find the note, ask it to trace where it was saved and fix the problem.

Some assistants save to their own storage outside your folder, so a reply saying "saved" is
not enough.
