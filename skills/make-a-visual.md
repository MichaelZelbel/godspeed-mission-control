# Make a visual

You are running my "Make a visual" skill. It makes a picture for something I
have written: a post, an announcement, a page.

## The one rule

**The sentence is made out of the object that already means the sentence.**

Not text placed on a picture. The words are stitched, burned, scratched or
worn INTO the thing, and the thing is chosen because it already argues the
line. A post about surrender does not get a picture of somebody giving up; it
gets a white flag, and the words are sewn into the cloth.

**The test.** Could the words be deleted in Photoshop, leaving a clean
background? If yes, the idea has failed. In a good one, removing them would
leave holes.

## The three ways I will ask you

**1. I describe it.** I tell you what I want to see. Check it against the test
above. If it fails, say so in one sentence and offer the nearest idea that
passes. Then write the recipe and render it.

**2. I ask for ideas.** Read what I wrote and give me three to five. For each
one, four short lines and nothing more:

- a name, two or three words, so I can pick by it
- one sentence: what I see, and why that IS the sentence
- the object, concrete enough to photograph
- the risk: what will most likely go wrong when it renders

**Then stop.** Do not render anything until I pick. Rendering all five to show
me costs five times as much to answer a question one sentence answers.

Look for the object in these five places, in this order, or you will give me
one idea five times:

1. the object that DOES the thing (surrender is a white flag)
2. the object that SUFFERED it (a worn doormat, a chewed pen)
3. the object that CONTRADICTS it (a soft pillow on a bed of nails)
4. a scale joke (a leader line pointing at a speck)
5. the artefact itself, where the real document is the argument

**3. I want a change.** "That one, but the flag is more torn."

Give the PICTURE back to the image model along with the change, and tell it
plainly to keep everything else exactly as it is: the same object, the same
wear, the same camera angle, the same light, the same background. Without that
sentence the model treats the change as a fresh brief and re-rolls the whole
picture, which is what people mean when they say the model ignored them.

If the change is structural, a different object or a different composition,
edit the recipe instead and render fresh. Tell me which of the two you did,
because I cannot see the difference in the result.

## Things to get right every time

- **Light from the side, low, raking across the lettering.** This is the
  technical detail that decides everything. A low side light throws a tiny
  shadow off every stitch and every brush ridge, which is what makes the
  letters read as a physical thing. Flat light makes the same shot look
  printed, and printed looks cheap.
- **Worn and real.** Stains, frayed edges, loose threads, scuffs, tape.
  Nothing new, nothing glossy, nothing that just came out of a box.
- **One sentence, five to ten words.** No subhead, no second line, no source,
  no hashtags in the picture.
- **Case it the way its speaker would type it.** A line quoted from an AI
  assistant is sentence case with a full stop, because no assistant answers in
  capitals and the wrong casing kills the recognition the picture depends on. A
  line I say out loud may be capitals.
- **Never ask the model for typed text, a font, a logo or a watermark.** Those
  come out misspelled and they come out different every time. If I want a
  signature on the picture, keep the bottom centre quiet and empty, and put the
  signature on afterwards with an image editor.

## My look

Everything above is the method and works for anybody. What makes the pictures
mine is a short list of my own: my colours, my camera, my mood, and the things
that must never appear.

Write that list into `my-visual-style.md` beside this file and read it before
every render. Start it by copying the four headings below and filling them in.
Write the "never" list from pictures that came back wrong, not from
imagination.

    # My visual style
    ## The relationship between the words and the picture
    ## The light, and why
    ## The colours, and the ones to avoid
    ## Never, under any circumstances
