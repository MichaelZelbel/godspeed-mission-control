# Ideas

One file per idea: `ideas/<short-name>.md`. An idea is a concrete thing that could be made or
done for one of your goals: a tool, a page, a list, a free product, a use case, a person worth
teaming up with, an experiment.

Your hub adds to this folder every day, from three places: what you say out loud ("why don't
we..."), what it reads in the world (who visits, what people ask for, what already works), and
what the people it models actually shipped. You never have to repeat an idea. Say it once and it
is here the same day, in your words.

Every file starts like this:

    GOAL: <the goal it serves>
    STATUS: open | chosen | made | dropped
    SOURCE: <whose idea, and the date>
    WHAT IT IS: <two sentences>
    WHO GETS WHAT: <a stranger, or you>
    SMALLEST VERSION: <what one work run can finish>
    OUTSIDE NUMBER: <what would show it worked, read where, by when>
    NEEDS FROM YOU: <nothing, or the one thing>
    RISK: <what could go wrong>
    JUDGMENT: <the hub's honest view, including how to make it better than it was said>

Each morning the hub ranks the open ideas for every goal it is working on, picks the best, and
makes the smallest version that day. When the outside number comes in, it is written here, and it
raises or kills the ideas next to it. A dropped idea stays, with the reason.

## How an idea gets in

An idea is written here only after it was researched from something read that day and then
attacked three ways. The hub does this itself, every day, as one of its own jobs.

**Research.** One area per hunt. Every candidate names ONE specific thing of yours and a specific
defect or opening read today: an address, a number, a quote, a missing thing. No evidence, no
idea. The smallest version must fit one work session. Advice that would fit anybody ("post more
often") is not an idea.

**Attack 1: is the evidence true?** Read the address, the number or the file again. A wrong
number, a dead link, a defect that is already fixed, or a thing that already exists kills it.

**Attack 2: can the hub really do it alone?** Does the login, the tool, the route really exist
today? Does "needs nothing from you" tell the truth? New public words under your name, or anything
sent to another person, is one yes from you, and the idea says so.

**Attack 3: does it survive your hardest question?** "How do you know this works?" "How does this
bring me closer to the goal?" "Why this and not something better?" Is the claimed effect honest
in size? Could it harm you?

Then the score, 1 to 10: how certain it is to work at the size claimed, times how much it
matters. An idea scored 3 or lower is not written. Four lessons from the first hunt, 2026-09-20:
polish on a page nobody visits is worth nothing; effects are small (a link under a video is
clicked about once per 750 views); check that the route exists before promising it; and search
whether a better-known free thing already does it.
